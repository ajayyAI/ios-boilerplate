# Code of Conduct

## The standard

Be decent to people. Argue about code, not about the person writing it.

Concretely, in issues, pull requests, reviews and any other project space:

- **Critique the work.** "This leaks the task on teardown" is useful. "This is
  amateur work" is not — it says nothing a contributor can act on.
- **Assume competence.** Someone asking an obvious question has usually already
  read the docs and found them unclear. That is a bug in the docs.
- **Accept a "no".** Not every contribution fits the project's direction, and not
  every review comment has to be litigated to exhaustion.
- **Respect the person.** Harassment, discrimination, sustained hostility, sexual
  attention, doxxing, and deliberate intimidation are out — regardless of who the
  target is or how good the patch attached to them is.

This applies wherever you are representing the project, including spaces we do not
control.

## Enforcement

Report a problem privately to the maintainers listed in [`CODEOWNERS`](CODEOWNERS).
Include links and enough context to act on. Reports stay between you and the people
handling them; we will not name you to the person reported without asking first.

Maintainers respond in proportion to what happened, and will say which of these
they are applying:

| Response | When |
|---|---|
| A private word | A first slip, usually unintentional |
| A public correction | It happened in public and the record needs fixing |
| A temporary ban | A pattern, or a single serious incident |
| A permanent ban | Sustained behaviour after a ban, or harassment of an individual |

Maintainers who ignore this document are held to it like anyone else. Removing
someone's comment, commit or issue is a normal moderation action, not a punishment,
and does not require a report first.

Acting in bad faith — retaliating against a reporter, or filing a report to punish
someone — is itself a violation.
